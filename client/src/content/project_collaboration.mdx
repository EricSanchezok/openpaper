Research often involves several people, but that does not mean every part of the workflow should become a group chat.

Scholens Projects use a deliberately small collaboration model: one owner invites collaborators into a shared paper collection. The Project is the common research context; each person's AI conversations remain private.

## What a Project shares

Collaborators can work from the same:

- paper collection
- highlights and annotations created in the Project
- citation artifacts
- Audio Overviews
- Data Tables

Research outputs are shared with Project collaborators by default. Their creator can hide or reshare an output, and the Project owner can moderate any output. Annotations inherit the visibility of their parent highlight, so a note and its quoted evidence cannot disagree about who can see them.

## What stays private

Project conversations are personal workspaces. Other collaborators cannot open, list, or continue your chats, even though those chats use the same Project papers as context.

This boundary is intentional:

- the paper collection is shared evidence
- research outputs are shareable results
- prompts, drafts, and conversational history belong to the person who created them

When a chat produces a useful citation artifact, the artifact can appear in the shared Project without exposing the private conversation or message that produced it.

## Permissions without roles

Scholens does not assign collaborators broad Viewer, Editor, or Admin roles. The owner grants each collaborator only the abilities they need:

| Ability | What it allows |
|---|---|
| **Edit Project** | Change the Project title and description |
| **Manage papers** | Add and remove papers from the shared collection |
| **Manage collaborators** | Invite, remove, and configure other collaborators |

Every collaborator can read the Project and contribute private conversations and shared research outputs. Permissions only govern management actions.

A collaborator who can manage access cannot grant powers they do not have or modify someone whose permissions exceed their own. Project deletion and ownership transfer remain owner-only actions.

## Inviting a collaborator

1. Open the Project.
2. Open its collaborator settings.
3. Enter the collaborator's email.
4. Select the specific management abilities they need.
5. Send the invitation.

An invitation expires after seven days. If the inviter loses the authority to grant those abilities before the invitation is accepted, Scholens rejects the invitation instead of silently escalating access.

## Ownership and quotas

The Project owner is responsible for shared Project resources:

- papers and storage count against the owner's resource limits
- Project-level background work reserves the owner's applicable resource capacity
- transferring ownership verifies that the new owner can carry the Project before the transfer completes

AI conversations are different. Token Credits are charged to the person who actually triggers the model request because conversations are private and personal.

If a collaborator leaves or is removed, the shared Project remains intact and stops appearing in that person's workspace. Their private conversations remain private and are detached from the Project if the Project itself is deleted.

## A practical workflow

1. The owner creates a Project and adds the initial papers.
2. Collaborators expand or curate the collection if they have paper-management access.
3. Each person investigates the shared evidence through private conversations.
4. Useful highlights, annotations, citations, audio, and tables become part of the shared research layer.
5. A creator can hide an unfinished output until it is ready to share.

This keeps collaboration useful without turning Scholens into a heavy team-management system: share the evidence and the results, preserve the privacy of the thinking process.
